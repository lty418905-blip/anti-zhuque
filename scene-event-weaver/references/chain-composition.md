# Causal event-chain composition

Use a chain only when a root incident must produce several visible consequences before it can settle. A chain is not a list of unrelated surprises.

## Required logic

1. Freeze one root trigger.
2. Give it a real pressure window if time matters.
3. Write two to six visible steps.
4. Connect every pair through `state_out → state_in`.
5. Preserve at least one plausible abort option the character does not take, with a reason.
6. Allow no more than one independent coincidence after the root.
7. End at a visible settlement point.
8. Carry residue into at least one later action.

Delete any middle step as a test. If the next step still happens unchanged, the handshake is false and the chain is only a montage.

## Root-seeded writer mode

The writer receives the root, goal, pressure window, step range, abort options, risk ceiling, forbidden results, and existing scene facts. The writer may construct direct consequences but may not read the full library, draw a second event, or add a plot-level result.

After drafting, extract actual steps and compare them with the contract. Reject chains that rely on repeated coincidence, sudden incompetence, serious injury for comedy, unearned intimacy, institutional impossibility, or a new scientific or factual claim.

## Common failures

- `CHAIN_BY_AND_THEN`: steps are adjacent, not causal.
- `RESET_AFTER_EVENT`: residue vanishes in the next paragraph.
- `COMEDY_ESCALATION_WITHOUT_CEILING`: harm grows only to keep the joke alive.
- `SECOND_ROOT_SMUGGLED_IN`: a new accident reconnects the chain.
- `PLOT_SHORTCUT`: the chain solves the central problem by chance.
- `RELATION_UPGRADE`: ordinary help becomes proof of love, loyalty, or therapy.
